Since 2021, Marvel fans have argued about the ship of Theseus. If you replace every plank of a ship, is it still the same ship? Is the White Vision still Vision? In Vision Quest episode 2, Ultron finally answers the question by pointing out that everyone has been asking the wrong one.
This is a full spoiler review of episode 2, Theseus, which dropped alongside episode 1. Spoilers for both episodes. The short version, this one is every bit as good as the premiere, and the best moment in it is also the one decision I'd change. Let's get into it.

Quick history lesson first. The ship of Theseus comes from Greek writer Plutarch in his life of Theseus. He wrote that the Athenians preserved the ship Theseus sailed home on, swapping out old planks for new ones as they rotted, until philosophers started arguing over whether it was still the same ship. That's the debate the two Visions had in the WandaVision finale.
Ultron calls the whole debate garbage. He says it skips the part of the story that matters. Theseus sailed that ship to Crete to kill the Minotaur and bring the children of Athens home. Nobody preserved the ship because of its wood. They preserved it because of what the man did with it. So, his advice is simple. When someone has your son, don't be the ship, be the king.
That's the same honest Ultron from episode one. He isn't telling Vision what he wants to hear. He's telling him the thing nobody else in the house will say.
Jarvis pushes back. He warns Vision to keep his emotions in check, and points to a portrait of the first of their kind, a cautionary tale in flames. That's Jim Hammond, the original Human Torch. Comics lesson, he's an android built by Professor Phineas Horton, created by Carl Burgos, and debuted in Marvel Comics issue number one in October of 1939. He's one of the very first Marvel heroes, and the show just made him the family's founding ancestor.
Paladin has taken Tommy to St. Mabon's, the same institution that prison bus was taking him to earlier. Edith scouts the lobby through an old View-Master, spotting tactical boots instead of sneakers and earpieces instead of earrings. It's a trap.
Vision walks in anyway with John Denver's Calypso playing, and he's as brutal here as we've ever seen him. He tears through Paladin's mercenaries. He finds Tommy strapped down, bathed in red light, and to Tommy that's the red-faced demon from his nightmares. Then the real trap springs. A field that shuts down Vision's systems. Paladin walks in with a shotgun loaded with adamantium rounds.
And here's my one gripe with this episode. Paladin levels a shotgun at him and Vision just walks into every shot. No charge, no dodge, no phasing, from a guy who can make himself intangible, even with the trap draining him. It's hard to watch the most powerful Avenger in the room stroll into adamantium buckshot. He survives because Paladin runs out of ammo, not because of anything Vision does.
What comes next is my favorite moment of the episode, and the one that I'd change. Vision is failing. If his body dies, everyone in the mindscape dies with him, and there isn't enough power to keep them all running. Ultron, of all people, says that if there was ever a moment for a noble sacrifice, this is it.

It's beautiful. It's also the wrong AI, and I know exactly why the writers picked him. If Dummy went out, it wouldn't hurt us like this. Jarvis is the big brother, the one who came first. Lose Jarvis, and you lose the guard with Ultron still inside. And remember who pitched the idea of a sacrifice in the first place.
So yes, it's my favorite moment in the episode. I also think it's going to cost Vision more than the show is letting on. And Ultron's reaction is pure Ultron. Jarvis certainly wasn't his first choice. Honest to the end, even about who he'd rather see deleted.
